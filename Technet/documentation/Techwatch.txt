TECHWATCH PROJECT SCOPE
=======================

Status: Concept and hardware validation
Project: Techpack / Technet
Related systems: Ruppert-lite, PackService, TechOS (working name)


1. PURPOSE
----------

Techwatch is a low-power, wearable Technet endpoint. It provides a
glanceable user interface, collects personal and device telemetry, and
acts as a trusted control and notification surface for the wider Techpack
network.

The watch should be useful while disconnected from the rest of Technet and
should synchronize buffered state and telemetry when a trusted connection is
available. It is not intended to replace Ruppert-lite or a more capable host.
Where local resources are insufficient, it should forward requests to a
trusted Ruppert-lite device and clearly indicate that delegation to the user.


2. PRIMARY GOALS
---------------

### Technet integration

- Discover nearby Techpack devices and display their availability.
- Enroll as a new Techpack member through an administrator-approved,
    physically confirmed setup flow.
- Maintain a persistent device identity and report capabilities, health, and
    service status.
- Send authorized commands to other devices through the Technet message bus.
- Receive device state, alerts, and responses from Technet.
- Communicate with Ruppert-lite through a lightweight watch client or a
    delegated request interface on a more capable device.
- Provide a visible indication of connection state, synchronization state,
    and whether a response was produced locally or remotely.

### Notifications and user interaction

- Display prioritized notifications, alerts, and command results.
- Support acknowledge, dismiss, snooze, and retry actions.
- Preserve a small local notification history when disconnected.
- Provide configurable vibration, sound, and display behavior where hardware
    supports it.
- Provide an emergency or high-priority alert action with explicit confirmation
    for actions that could affect other devices or expose personal data.

### Health and activity monitoring

Collect, timestamp, and report supported measurements. Sensor availability
must be capability-driven; unsupported measurements must be reported as
unavailable rather than represented as false values.

- Heart rate and heart-rate trends.
- Step count and general activity.
- Motion, orientation, and stationary/activity state.
- Sleep and rest periods, where the available sensors and battery permit it.
- Skin or ambient temperature, clearly labeled by sensor source.
- Blood oxygen, only if a suitable sensor is present and validated.
- Optional health-related derived metrics such as hydration reminders,
    activity goals, and estimated energy expenditure.
- Sensor quality, calibration state, sampling interval, and sensor errors.

Techwatch must present health information as measurements and trends, not as
medical diagnoses.

### Watch interface

- Provide a watch-specific UI integrated with TechOS (name to be finalized).
- Provide a glanceable home screen with time, battery, connection state, and
    selected health or system metrics.
- Provide screens for notifications, health, connected devices, commands,
    settings, and diagnostics.
- Support touch input, display sleep/wake, and an interaction model suitable
    for a small screen.
- Keep core screens usable in low-power mode and when Technet is unavailable.
- Include accessibility settings such as text size, contrast, haptic feedback,
    and reduced-motion behavior where supported.


3. OFFLINE AND RELIABILITY REQUIREMENTS
---------------------------------------

- Continue collecting local telemetry without a network connection.
- Buffer telemetry and event records locally until synchronization is possible.
- Mark records with timestamps, source, quality, and synchronization state.
- Queue safe, user-approved commands for later delivery only when explicitly
    requested; do not silently replay sensitive commands.
- Recover cleanly after power loss, reset, or an interrupted synchronization.
- Provide low-power, normal, and emergency power modes.
- Warn before battery exhaustion and preserve critical logs during shutdown.
- Expose degraded state when a sensor, service, storage area, or connection is
    unavailable.
- Use a watchdog or equivalent recovery mechanism for stalled services.


4. SECURITY AND PRIVACY REQUIREMENTS
------------------------------------

- Use device enrollment and identity verification before normal participation
    in Technet.
- Keep private identity keys on the watch and never transmit them.
- Use authenticated, encrypted connections for trusted device communication.
- Enforce capability-based permissions for commands, configuration, health
    data, and enrollment operations.
- Require local confirmation for high-risk commands and new-device enrollment.
- Reject replayed, malformed, unauthorized, or unverifiable commands.
- Protect health, location, behavior, and notification data from unnecessary
    synchronization.
- Provide lock, disconnect, revoke, and recovery behavior for a lost or stolen
    watch.
- Record security-relevant events such as enrollment, failed authentication,
    rejected commands, policy changes, and recovery actions.


5. TELEMETRY AND DATA MODEL
---------------------------

Each record should include, where applicable:

- Schema version.
- Event or measurement timestamp and device clock status.
- Persistent device ID and user-facing alias.
- Data source and sensor identifier.
- Measurement value, unit, and sampling interval.
- Quality, confidence, calibration state, or degradation flags.
- Local sequence number and synchronization status.
- Privacy classification and retention policy.

The watch should expose the following logical data groups:

- Device identity and capabilities.
- Battery, charging, power mode, and estimated remaining time.
- Thermal state and internal temperature sensors.
- Runtime state, uptime, boot or reset reason, and firmware version.
- Network discovery, connection, heartbeat, and synchronization state.
- Health and activity measurements.
- Notifications, commands, acknowledgements, and audit events.
- Storage usage, queue depth, and service health.


6. HARDWARE BASELINE
--------------------

Initial target: LilyGo T-Display-S3 Touch version.

- ESP32-S3 display and microcontroller platform.
- Wi-Fi and Bluetooth connectivity.
- 1.9-inch touchscreen display.
- 16 MB flash.
- 8 MB RAM.
- 3.3 V power supply.
- Arduino IDE and MicroPython development options.

Prototype battery: 
Ultra-Thin LiPo Battery LP284362 3.7V 800mAh
LP284362 3.7V@800mAh 2.96Wh
with Protection Circuit & Wires AWG26

Dimension: 3,2 x 43 x 63mm

Hardware-specific goals:

- Identify the exact display, touch controller, battery, charging circuit,
    sensors, and available GPIO interfaces before implementation is finalized.
- Define a capability manifest so software can adapt to optional sensors and
    hardware revisions.
- Measure realistic battery life for display-on, idle, disconnected, and
    sensor-intensive workloads.
- Reserve storage for firmware, configuration, buffered telemetry, logs, and
    recovery data.
- Keep the first implementation within the memory and flash limits of the
    target board.

Li-Pro Charging Module: MCP73831
-lighter than alternative options


7. SYSTEM SERVICES AND INTEGRATION POINTS
-----------------------------------------

The watch should integrate with the existing service model where practical:

- `tp-netlink`: discovery, device presence, heartbeat, and capabilities.
- `tp-msgbus`: commands, responses, notifications, and telemetry transport.
- `tp-authlite`: identity, enrollment, session tokens, and permissions.
- `tp-packboard`: wearable and environmental sensor ingestion.
- `tp-healthstats`: health trends and derived activity metrics.
- `tp-syncd`: selective synchronization and offline queue handling.
- `tp-ruppertlog`: local and distributed event logging.
- `tp-configd`: device profile, power mode, and policy state.
- `tp-notify`: notification routing and presentation rules.
- `tp-updater`: signed application or service updates where supported.

The watch must remain functional if any nonessential service is unavailable.


8. INITIAL DELIVERY PHASES
--------------------------

### Phase 1: Hardware and local prototype

- Boot, display, touch input, timekeeping, battery reporting, and basic power
    states.
- Local UI shell and diagnostic screen.
- Hardware capability detection.

### Phase 2: Technet endpoint

- Discovery, enrollment, authenticated connection, heartbeat, and device
    status reporting.
- Notification receipt and basic command request/response flow.

### Phase 3: Telemetry and offline operation

- Sensor ingestion, timestamped local storage, buffering, synchronization,
    quality flags, and health/activity views.

### Phase 4: Hardening and maintenance

- Recovery behavior, audit events, lock/revocation flows, power profiling,
    update delivery, retention controls, and broader hardware validation.


9. OUT OF SCOPE FOR THE FIRST PROTOTYPE
---------------------------------------

- Medical diagnosis or clinical monitoring.
- Fully independent AI inference on the watch.
- Unattended execution of high-risk remote commands.
- Continuous high-rate sensing without a measured battery and storage budget.
- Cloud dependency for core operation.
- Support for every possible watch sensor before the baseline hardware is
    validated.


10. OPEN DESIGN DECISIONS
-------------------------

- Final operating environment: MicroPython, Arduino-based firmware, or a
    dedicated lightweight runtime.
- Whether Bluetooth is used for provisioning, primary transport, or both.
- Exact sensors available on the selected board and any external sensor board.
- Local data retention duration and health-data synchronization policy.
- Authentication and local unlock mechanism.
- Notification priority rules and emergency behavior.
- Final TechOS name and UI toolkit.
- Whether boot-device enrollment is performed directly by the watch or by a
    host-assisted setup flow.
